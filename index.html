The gap

How well do we actually know Tbilisi's street dogs?

The city counts them. We want to understand them. Where each dog ranges, who it travels with, how its body condition shifts with the seasons, how its life unfolds year over year.

A real public dataset on Tbilisi's free-roaming dogs does not exist yet. This platform builds it, one sighting at a time.

How it works

From sighting to story, in six steps.

1

Spot a street dog

Anywhere in the city. The dog you walk past every morning, the one sleeping by the cafe, a new face in the courtyard.

2

Read the ear tag

Ear-tag IDs are how individual dogs are identified across sightings. Snap the tag and the app's OCR fills the number in for you.

3

Photograph and describe

Take a picture of the dog and add what you can see, body condition, size, and mood. It only takes a few seconds.

4

Flag or follow up (optional)

Report injuries or issues to local dog organisations, or pull up adoption information if you want to take things further.

5

Peek into their lives

Browse the same dog over weeks and months. Where it lives, who it spends time with, how it changes. The secret lives of street dogs, becoming a little less secret.

6

Join a research mission

Pick a district that needs coverage and help us build its dataset, one walk at a time. The app shows you which streets still need eyes.

Local partners

Who looks after the dogs.

Dog welfare in Tbilisi runs through a small set of organisations. If a dog needs capture, shelter, treatment, or a new home, these are the people on the ground.

Government · Tbilisi municipality

AMA · Animal Monitoring Agency

Controls Tbilisi's stray animal population and ensures public safety. Responds to 112 reports, captures aggressive or vulnerable strays, runs the municipal shelter, and handles vaccination, sterilization, microchipping, and pet registration.

ama.gov.ge
NGO · rescue & rehabilitation

DogHome

Rescues and treats severely injured dogs from across Georgia, places them with families locally and abroad (especially Germany), and advocates against cruelty in state-run shelters.

Research questions

What we're trying to find out.

The platform is built around questions that have been hard to answer at scale. Every logged sighting nudges one of them forward.

Territory & movement

How far does an individual dog actually range?

Repeated sightings of the same tagged dog reveal real home ranges, far better than one-off surveys.

Group dynamics

Which dogs travel together, and for how long?

Co-occurrence patterns across thousands of photos let us infer pack structure and turnover.

Body condition

How does health track with seasons and food sources?

Standardised condition scoring builds a seasonal health curve for each city we cover.

TNR response

What happens to a population after a sterilisation programme?

Long-term sighting trends quantify whether interventions actually change the picture on the ground.

Longitudinal welfare

How does one dog's life change over months and years?

Per-dog timelines combine sightings, vet notes, and adoption events into a real biography.

Human-dog interaction

Where are conflict zones, and where are safe zones?

Mapping reported incidents alongside sightings helps cities place resources where they matter.

Built for the long view

Ongoing monitoring, not snapshots.

Each dog gets a persistent profile. The app's OCR reads ear tags to re-identify individuals across sightings, so health, body condition, and movement build into a real life history.

The longer the platform runs, the sharper the dataset becomes. Every new entry helps the older ones make sense.

The loop

Data that turns into care.

A dataset is only as useful as what gets done with it. Three feedback loops sit at the heart of the platform.

TNR coordination

Programmes hit the streets that need them.

Hot-spot maps from real sightings show shelters and city teams where sterilisation campaigns will have the most impact.

Vet alerts

Injured dogs reach rescuers fast.

Sightings flagged with visible injuries or illness route to local rescue networks in near real time.

Adoption pipelines

Documented dogs find homes.

A documented life history makes adoption dramatically easier, whether the new home is across the street or across the border.
